I went back to Alley Spring in December, which is not when most people go. The swimming hole was empty. The campground was closed. The mill parking area held exactly two other cars when I arrived.

What you get in December is the spring without the summer context. The water temperature stays around 58 degrees year-round, and the color doesn't change either — that particular turquoise from limestone chemistry and clarity. But in winter you see the spring itself more directly, without the green vegetation softening the edges. The blue-green water running over pale gravel, the bare trees on the bluffs above, the red mill standing in the gray light: a different composition.

The NPS keeps the site accessible year-round. The mill exterior is always visible, and the grounds are open for walking even when visitor facilities are closed. In December the Jacks Fork River downstream was running clear and low.

Alley Spring rewards multiple visits for exactly this reason. The summer version is the famous one, with the swimming and heat and crowds. But the winter version has something the summer visit doesn't: the chance to be there quietly, with nothing competing for your attention except the water running cold and clear over the rock.
