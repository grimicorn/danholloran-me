Rocky Falls in February is a different animal than Rocky Falls in July, and I mean that as a compliment to February. In summer the shut-ins draw crowds, and rightfully so. But in winter you get the geology without the company.

The rhyolite rock that defines this place is approximately 1.5 billion years old, part of the ancient St. Francois Mountain volcanic complex. Shut-ins form where fast-moving streams encounter this dense igneous rock, creating chutes and pools that look like they belong somewhere more remote and dramatic.

In February the water runs lower and clearer than summer flow, and you can see the structure of the rock more clearly. The red and gray tones of the rhyolite stand out against the pale winter light. The pool below the falls was glassy and dark and very cold. Ice had formed on the shaded rock faces on the north side of the creek.

The Ozark Trail runs through here in both directions — three miles south to Klepzig Mill, longer stretches north. In winter those trails are nearly empty and the creek crossings are low enough to manage easily. Rocky Falls Shut-in as a winter hiking destination is something more people should know about.
