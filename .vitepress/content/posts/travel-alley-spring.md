I've been to Alley Spring in summer, when the swimming hole below the mill is full and children are splashing in water so cold it makes them shriek. I've been in fall, when the hardwoods above the bluffs turn orange and the spring branch runs brilliant green-blue against the fallen leaves on the banks. Both visits were right.

The red mill — built in 1894, painted that specific barn red that looks almost theatrical — is the anchor of the place. The National Park Service operates it as a living history site within the Ozark National Scenic Riverways, and inside you can see the turbine system and milling machinery intact and original. The miller's house and a one-room schoolhouse are preserved nearby, remnants of the small community that once orbited the mill.

Alley Spring itself flows from a cave in the limestone bluffs at a consistent rate year-round, never freezing, always cold, always that impossible blue-green color that photographers keep failing to capture. The water temperature stays around 58 degrees, which means the spring branch below the mill is one of the better swimming holes in the Ozarks even in the middle of July heat.

The half-mile walk from the parking area to the mill is gentle and shaded. The campground nearby is one of the better ones in the Riverways. If you're doing an Ozarks trip and haven't built Alley Spring into the itinerary, put it there. It's one of those places that becomes a reference point for everything else.
