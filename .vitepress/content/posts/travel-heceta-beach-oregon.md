Heceta Beach is north of Florence, Oregon, where the Oregon Dunes National Recreation Area begins and the coastline opens into the kind of wide, flat beach that makes the Pacific look like it extends without end. The beach is long and sandy — no boulder fields interrupting the walk — and the wind comes in without much to stop it from the direction of Japan.

The Oregon Dunes stretch roughly 45 miles from Heceta Beach south to Cape Arago, 31,000 acres of sand that constitute the largest expanse of coastal sand dunes in North America. At Heceta Beach you're at the northern edge of that system, and the scale of what's behind the foredune becomes apparent when you walk up into it: hills of sand 500 feet high, sculpted by wind into forms that change with every significant weather event.

The beach itself is better for walking and kite-flying than swimming — rip currents and debris from the ocean make the water hazardous. Dogs are welcome with leash. The beach has a particular quality on foggy mornings, when the visibility drops and the sound of the surf is the only reference point for direction or distance.

Florence, just south, has more going for it than its reputation suggests: good seafood, Sand Master Park (the first sandboard park in the world), and the Siuslaw River crossing that puts you in the Siuslaw National Forest. Heceta Beach is the coastal entry point to all of that, worth more than a drive-past.
