I made it back to Saint Mary's Glacier on a different day than my first visit, this time in October when the fall colors in the canyon below had turned and the ice field above had consolidated into a compact sheet of late-season snow. The trail up was empty in the early morning, the parking lot still half-full, and the lake at 11,000 feet was that particular shade of deep blue that high-elevation water produces when the light is right.

The glacier — technically a semi-permanent ice field, the distinction being that it no longer flows under its own weight — sits above a cirque carved by glacial action thousands of years ago. The lake below it, St. Mary Lake, is the meltwater collection point, and the stream that runs from the lake down the trail is cold enough in July to make you reconsider any plans involving wading.

What keeps me coming back is that combination of accessibility and altitude. An hour from Denver, a short hike, and you're standing in genuinely alpine terrain with a view that doesn't require days of approach. The snowboarders and skiers who come in summer are a regular feature — on my first visit in September a few were still working lines on the upper field while hikers picked their way around the margins.

The Instagram handle for this post says grimicornsparkles, which is also the handle I used when I first photographed this place. The lake comes out better in person than in any shot I've taken. That's consistently true of places worth returning to.
