You see Multnomah Falls from the highway before you fully process what you're looking at — a white stripe on a basalt cliff that seems too tall and too thin to be real. From the parking area, the full 620-foot drop snaps into focus: water spilling off the rim of the upper falls, catching the Benson Bridge at the midpoint, then crashing into the pool below. It's the kind of scene that makes you understand why this is the most visited natural recreation site in the entire Pacific Northwest.

Multnomah sits 30 miles east of Portland in the Columbia River Gorge, a 292,000-acre national scenic area that was carved by the Columbia River and shaped dramatically by the Missoula Floods — a series of catastrophic glacial outbursts at the end of the last ice age that sent walls of water hundreds of feet high through this corridor. The results are the basalt cliffs, the side gorges, and the dozens of waterfalls that drop off the Washington and Oregon sides of the gorge. Multnomah is the biggest and most famous, but Horsetail Falls and Latourell Falls are just a few miles down the historic highway and see a fraction of the crowds.

The Benson Bridge — built in 1925, connecting the two tiers of the falls — is the classic vantage point, and it's about a ten-minute walk from the visitor center. From there, a trail switchbacks up another 1.1 miles to the top of the upper falls, gaining 800 feet. It's steep and crowded, but the view from the overlook, with the Columbia River visible in the distance through the gorge, makes the climb worthwhile. The falls flow year-round, fed by rainwater and snowmelt, which means there's no bad time to visit. Winter brings ice formations on the rocks; spring brings the fullest flow; summer brings everyone else.

A timed entry permit is now required in peak season — check recreation.gov before you go and book a couple weeks out. The Columbia Gorge Express runs shuttles from Portland and Troutdale, which is a genuinely good option if you want to skip the parking chaos and just watch the gorge go by through the windows instead.
