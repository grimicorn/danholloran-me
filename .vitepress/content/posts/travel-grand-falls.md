Grand Falls in Joplin is the kind of place that locals take for granted and visitors can't quite believe is real. A 163-foot-wide ledge of solid chert drops 12 feet into Shoal Creek year-round — no springs feeding it seasonally, no dependence on heavy rainfall to be impressive. It just runs.

The chert rock is what makes Grand Falls unusual. Most waterfall ledges erode over time into something softer, but chert is hard enough to resist, which is why the falls have maintained their character while the creek below has carved away the softer downstream stone. The natural waterfall exists downstream of a man-made dam that helps supply Joplin's water, but the falls themselves are entirely the work of geology.

In summer the light hits the spray in late afternoon and the rock face goes dark and wet while the mist catches gold. I've come here enough times to have opinions about the best angles, which is not something I expected to develop about a waterfall in Joplin, Missouri. The wide shot from the east bank gives you the full span. The close approach from the rocks below the falls gives you the sound.

People fish here. People swim here, carefully — the current below the falls is stronger than it looks. Families picnic on the grass near the bank. It's an unpretentious place that knows what it is: Missouri's largest waterfall, free to visit, open every day.
