I used to drive past the signs for Laumeier Sculpture Park for months before I finally stopped. The name sounds formal, like a place that requires hushed voices and dress shoes, but the reality is the opposite — it's a sprawling 105 acres where massive steel and stone sculptures sit in meadows and forests, free to anyone who shows up.

That first visit, I wasn't prepared for how big everything would be. Tony Tasset's "The Eye" watches you from across an open field, a surreal 30-foot pupil that feels almost alive depending on the angle and the light. Alexander Liberman's "The Way" is an assembly of salvaged steel oil tanks painted blazing red — it shouldn't work architecturally and somehow it absolutely does. I spent more time with these pieces than I expected to, circling them, stepping back, trying to figure out what I was actually looking at.

Laumeier opened in 1976 and was one of the first large-scale outdoor sculpture parks in the country. The grounds include a 1917 Tudor stone mansion that's now an education center, and a proper indoor gallery. The permanent collection holds over 70 major works, and they rotate in temporary exhibitions throughout the year. Programs run constantly — yoga classes on summer mornings, teen art camps, family craft weekends. The park sees around 300,000 visitors a year, which surprised me; it never feels crowded.

The 1.4-mile walking trail connects most of the major pieces, but there are enough side paths and open fields that wandering without a map feels right. Bring a sandwich. Find a spot near the creek. Sit with a piece of art longer than you think you need to. That's the move here.
